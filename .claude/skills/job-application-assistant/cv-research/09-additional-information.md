# Additional Information

The evidence for rules 31 to 34 in `../10-cv-best-practices.md`.

## What to take from it

- **Languages** carry an honest level in a known scale, native first.
- **Military service** is described by the role and the skills it took, in civilian words.
- **Everything else earns its place for this posting:** a relevant certification, volunteering, an award with its scale. Interests appear only when they bear on the role.
- **No references.**

## Languages

- **LinkedIn's scale** has five levels: elementary, limited working, professional working, full professional, and native or bilingual. Source: [LinkedIn's field reference on Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/references/fields/languages).
- **Monster** defines each level by what the person can do at work and suggests pairing a level with an example of its use. **SEEK** lists native languages first and recognizes formal scales such as CEFR. Sources: [Monster](https://www.monster.com/career-advice/article/Flaunt-Your-Fluency), [SEEK](https://www.seek.com.au/career-advice/article/language-level-of-proficiency).
- **Harvard's template** puts language proficiency under skills and interests. Source: [Harvard](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/).
- Self-rated levels tend to run high, so the level must survive an interview in that language (rule 44).

## Military service

- **Israel's Ministry of Defense,** in its guide for discharged soldiers: don't just list the role; translate it into the skills it used, such as handling pressure, making decisions under uncertainty and coordinating events. Courses, distinctions earned in the army and scholarships all count. Source: [the ministry's guide](https://www.hachvana.mod.gov.il/ConsultationAndDirection/Employment/Pages/cv-writing.aspx).
- **Israeli CV guides** list the dates, the unit, the role, the responsibilities and the rank, and advise translating military roles into language a civilian employer understands. Sources: [AllJobs](https://www.alljobs.co.il/Campaigns/CVCenter/CVCenterGuide.htm), [Mako, for discharged soldiers](https://www.mako.co.il/pzm-soldiers/discharge/Article-0d11198686e0431006.htm).
- **Reserve duty** is a protected ground under Israel's equal opportunities law (see `11-israel.md`). No source was found on how to present it, so rule 32 follows from the rules on experience and the law.
- **The template** gives military service one line, `\cvinfo{Military Service}{...}`.

## Availability

- The template has an `Availability` line. No source covers it; rule 33 keeps it to what the profile states, and `/apply` addresses availability in the cover letter when the posting raises it.

## Volunteering

- **LinkedIn's 2011 survey** of US professionals: 41% of hiring managers consider volunteer work as valuable as paid work, and 20% have hired someone because of it. Yet 89% of professionals had volunteered and only 45% listed it. The survey is old. Source: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2011/09/20/still-not-putting-volunteer-work-on-your-resume).
- **The Ministry of Defense's guide:** volunteering shows initiative and is worth listing. Source: [the ministry's guide](https://www.hachvana.mod.gov.il/ConsultationAndDirection/Employment/Pages/cv-writing.aspx).

## Awards and certifications

- **Awards with their scale.** The Tech Interview Handbook includes only job-related awards, quantified, as in "2016 | Best All-Round Product out of 50 teams | Facebook Hackathon". Google's recruiters' own better version is "Won second place out of 50 teams in hackathon". Sources: [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/), [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/bill-murphy-jr/google-recruiters-say-these-5-resume-tips-including-x-y-z-formula-will-improve-your-odds-of-getting-hired-at-google.html).
- **Certifications** count as evidence of a skill, mainly early in a career, and less than experience. See `08-education.md`.

## Interests

- **They can trigger bias.** In Rivera and Tilcsik's 2016 field experiment, CVs sent to 316 law firm offices differed only in signals of social class, carried partly by activities such as sailing or classical music. Higher-class men got significantly more callbacks, while higher-class women were judged less committed and lost the advantage. Source: [Kellogg School of Management](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/2016/class-advantage-commitment-penalty-the-interplay-of-social-class).
- **When they help.** Harvard's template has a line for personal interests. The Ministry of Defense's guide, for those without professional experience, prefers original hobbies to generic ones ("בישול בירה, ריצה למרחקים ארוכים" over "צפייה בסרטים, קריאת ספרים"). Surveys on whether recruiters read interests are thin and inconsistent. Sources: [Harvard](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/), [the ministry's guide](https://www.hachvana.mod.gov.il/ConsultationAndDirection/Employment/Pages/cv-writing.aspx).
- Hence rule 34: interests only when they bear on the role, never a generic list.

## References

- **EY:** "Do provide references only when asked." **Harvard:** do not list references. Sources: [EY (PDF)](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-gl/careers/documents/ey_resume-dos-and-donts-dec-2020.pdf), [Harvard](https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/).
