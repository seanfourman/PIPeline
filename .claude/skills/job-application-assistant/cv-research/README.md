## How strong each source is

- **Studies and field experiments,** such as TheLadders' eye-tracking, ResumeGo's experiments, the Duke and University of Washington studies, the Israeli photo experiment and Rivera and Tilcsik's class-signal study: the strongest, though some are old or small.
- **Employers' own words,** from the careers sites and recruiters of Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, IBM, the banks and the Big Four, and from Workday's, Greenhouse's and Lever's documentation: what the people who decide say they want.
- **Surveys** (NACE, CareerBuilder, Resume Genius, LinkedIn): what hiring managers say, which isn't always what they do.
- **Vendor data** (Jobscan, Enhancv, Simplify): real data from companies that sell CV tools. It is not independent, and it mostly shows an association, not a cause.
- **Guides** (the Tech Interview Handbook, Harvard, Israeli job boards and government guides): practitioner consensus.

## What the research doesn't settle

- Whether numbers in bullets cause more interviews. Every employer asks for them, but no experiment isolates their effect.
- How much the job title alone moves interviews. The only numbers are vendor data, and they range from 3.5 to 10.6 times.
- Whether recruiters read summaries. Claims that they skip them have no clear source.
- How to present reserve duty: no source was found.
- Whether a photo helps: the studies disagree by gender and country.
- What Israel's low-tech employers (the health funds, banks and insurers) look for in a CV. None of them publishes guidance, so their postings and the rules are the guide.
