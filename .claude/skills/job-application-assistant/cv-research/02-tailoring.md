# Tailoring to the Posting

The evidence for rules 4 to 8 in `../10-cv-best-practices.md`.

## What to take from it

- Every required qualification the candidate meets must be visible, in the posting's words, with the preferred ones after them. This is what both the recruiters and the AI graders check first.
- Use the posting's own terms and, where honest, its title. Spell out an abbreviation once.
- Tailor every CV, but never mirror the posting: a CV that matches everything isn't believable.

## Required qualifications first

- **Meta:** "Review the minimum qualifications to ensure you're qualified for the position and your resume clearly reflects this." Source: [Meta's hiring process](https://www.metacareers.com/hiring-process/).
- **Amazon:** tailor each CV by the keywords in the posting's "Basic and Preferred Qualifications". An Amazon recruiter: "I would pick up to five positions that you feel you 100% satisfy the requirements within the basic qualifications listed", and the CV should reflect those skills. Sources: [4 ways to improve your resume](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-job-application-resume-writing-tips), [7 tips from recruiters](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/applying-at-amazon-7-tips-from-recruiters).
- **Workday's AI:** a candidate who meets every basic qualification gets an A or a B, and the preferred qualifications separate A from B. Source: [the August 2026 release note](https://doc.workday.com/hiredscore/en-us/workday-hiredscore/hiredscore-release-notes/2026/august/wednesday--5th-august.html).

## The posting's words

- **IBM:** "Look for the keywords in the job description and find a way to integrate those keywords into your resume." Its list of common mistakes starts with skills and experience that aren't aligned with the job. Sources: [IBM's recruiters](https://www.ibm.com/careers/blog/make-your-resume-stand-out-from-the-pile), [IBM's eight mistakes](https://www.ibm.com/careers/blog/eight-common-resume-writing-mistakes-that-keep-you-from-getting-your-dream-job).
- **Israeli recruiting managers** (WalkMe, Gloat, Cervello, accessiBe): use keywords from the posting, such as a specific programming language or project-management tool, and put the strongest matches in the top lines. Source: [Mako](https://www.mako.co.il/finances-money/Article-02c0a6072605581027.htm).
- **Exact strings.** Jobscan says most systems match keywords exactly and that some weigh frequency; its tool recommends matching at least 75% of a posting's terms. Vendor guidance. Sources: [Jobscan's tutorial](https://www.jobscan.co/jobscan-tutorial), [Jobscan on match rates](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/what-jobscan-match-rate-should-i-aim-for/).
- **Abbreviations and jargon.** The Tech Interview Handbook says to write out an abbreviation's full name once, such as "Amazon Web Services (AWS)". Google's recruiters say to use shorthand like "QoQ" only when you're sure the reviewer knows it, and EY says not to fill the experience section with industry jargon. Sources: [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/), [Inc. on Google's advice](https://www.inc.com/bill-murphy-jr/google-recruiters-say-these-5-resume-tips-including-x-y-z-formula-will-improve-your-odds-of-getting-hired-at-google.html), [EY (PDF)](https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-gl/careers/documents/ey_resume-dos-and-donts-dec-2020.pdf).

## The title

- In Jobscan's 2025 analysis of over 2.5 million applications made through its tools, CVs whose title matched the posting's had a 10.6 times higher interview rate. Vendor data, association only; an earlier Jobscan analysis of about a million applications put the effect at about 3.5 times. Sources: [Jobscan, 2025](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/interview-rates-study), [notes from Jobscan's 2024 talk](https://dandylyons.github.io/notes/Topics/Professional-Development/Job-Search/2024-HireReady/2024-09-05-HireReady-Keynote---1-Million-Job-Applications).
- Recruiters' eyes go to titles first, per TheLadders' eye-tracking. Source: [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/recruiters-spend-74-seconds-looking-at-your-resume/541582/).

## Tailor every CV

- **Google:** "You should write a new, tailored resume for every position you apply for." Source: [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/bill-murphy-jr/google-recruiters-say-these-5-resume-tips-including-x-y-z-formula-will-improve-your-odds-of-getting-hired-at-google.html).
- **Intel:** a one-size-fits-all CV makes it hard for a recruiter to spot the fit. **Microsoft:** tailor the CV to the role and the company. Sources: [Intel](https://blogs.intel.com/jobs/2015/02/5-quick-tips-effective-resume), [Microsoft Life](https://news.microsoft.com/life/make-resume-shine/).
- **Amazon** differs a little: "You don't need a completely different resume for each role you apply to", only one that emphasizes the matching experience. The system tailors each CV anyway. Source: [Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/amazon-job-application-resume-writing-tips).
- **Israel:** never a generic CV sent "blind" (Gloat); a long, cluttered CV suggests an unfocused candidate (accessiBe). Source: [Mako](https://www.mako.co.il/finances-money/Article-02c0a6072605581027.htm).

## Never mirror

- `user/CV_RULES.md` limits the additions beyond the profile and forbids mirroring the posting, because a CV that ticks every requirement isn't believable. Jobscan's own guidance warns against claims you can't back up in an interview. Source: [Jobscan on summaries](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/resume-summary/).
- The posting is the target, not the source: everything on the CV comes from the profile.

## Several roles at one employer

- The Tech Interview Handbook warns against applying to many roles at the same company, since a recruiter may not tell which one you want. Amazon suggests up to five roles you fully qualify for. Nvidia recommends the top three to five, and its university recruiter two or three. Meta allows several applications but keeps one CV per applicant for all of them, so that one CV has to cover every role applied for there. Sources: [Tech Interview Handbook](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/resume/), [Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/workplace/applying-at-amazon-7-tips-from-recruiters), [Nvidia](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/about-nvidia/careers/how-we-hire/), [Nvidia's blog](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-life-linh-nguyen/), [Meta](https://www.metacareers.com/hiring-process/).
- The system shows this as a recommendation on the settings screen, beside the daily run, and sets no limit: the user's decision on 2026-11-05.

## Beyond the CV

- **Referrals.** By Jobvite's data, about 30% of referred candidates are hired against about 7% of other applicants. In Jobscan's 2025 report, 69.4% of recruiters rate referrals as very or extremely important. Sources: [The Undercover Recruiter](https://theundercoverrecruiter.com/infographic-employee-referrals-hire), [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/interview-rates-study).
- **Cover letters.** In ResumeGo's experiment, applications got 10.7% callbacks with no letter, 12.5% with a generic one and 16.4% with a tailored one. In Resume Genius's 2023 survey, 83% of hiring managers said they read them, while Google's recruiters say Google very rarely asks for or reads one. The system writes one when asked. Sources: [ResumeGo](https://resumego.net/research/cover-letters), [Resume Genius](https://resumegenius.com/blog/cover-letter-help/cover-letter-statistics), [Inc.](https://www.inc.com/bill-murphy-jr/google-recruiters-say-these-5-resume-tips-including-x-y-z-formula-will-improve-your-odds-of-getting-hired-at-google.html).
- **LinkedIn.** In Jobscan's 2025 data, an optimized LinkedIn profile went with a 2.2 times higher interview rate. Israeli recruiters say to assume the employer will look the candidate up on LinkedIn, so the profile must match the CV. Sources: [Jobscan](https://www.jobscan.co/blog/interview-rates-study), [Calcalist](https://www.calcalist.co.il/articles/0,7340,L-3674552,00.html), [Mako](https://www.mako.co.il/finances-money/Article-02c0a6072605581027.htm).
