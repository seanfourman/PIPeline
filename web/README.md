# Web interface

A local interface for everything in this workspace. Double-click `start-web.cmd` in the repository root. It opens at `http://127.0.0.1:4174`, and the window must stay open while you use it.

## How it is built

The interface is React, written in `ui/`. The server bundles it into `public/build/` on every start, so there is no separate build step. Text is in `ui/i18n.ts`, in Hebrew and English, and `ui.test.ts` checks that both have the same keys. Pip, the companion, is an abstract fox in big pixel blocks in `ui/pip.tsx`, and the home screen's banner is the login's forest at rest, drawn by `paintStill` in `ui/intro.ts`. The banner is the one part of the app in pixel art, with a board and the Handjet pixel font like the login's, and at its foot the forest dissolves into the page in a dither of pixel blocks. Along the journey at its foot Pip stands on the sign of the stage you are at; the browser remembers the stage it was last seen on, so a stage reached while you were elsewhere shows as Pip hopping on to it, a stage at a time. The jobs page is a single search box over a moving forest-rainbow glow, with the first few of your roles in a straight row of liquid-glass buttons and a + that unfolds the rest downward in a curved fan like a Dock stack. The labels open one after another and fold back in reverse order, curving inward in either language. The fan never scrolls: each column holds only what fits above the window's bottom, and the rest start another column further in. It closes on selection, an outside click, Escape or tabbing away. Arrow keys, Home and End move through the roles; reduced motion skips the animation. At the box's end, the search settings (where, which opens a window to change the markets and areas right there, each area's rating a dropdown in the rating's own colour, and the search's options as rows of round choices, kept with the settings as `quickSearch`) and a colour-turning sparkles button for the two searches that use Claude, and once you type, a search button flies in. When the first search of a visit starts, the box and the roles rise to the top; Pip rides up on the box for a moment, frightened and shivering, then leaps down to the bottom of the window and runs off its edge, and the results rise in one after another, as a plain list like a search engine's: each with the company's initial on a round tile in a forest colour, the title, company and place, chips for how long ago it was posted and its area, and round buttons to open the posting and to check it and apply. Under the pointer, or reached by the keyboard, a button's name floats over it a moment later, in a small white pill like the menus, which the page draws (not the browser's tooltip). While the search runs, the middle of the page shows only Pip's head mark in grey, breathing, inside a thin turning ring. It has no words, just a label for screen readers, and fades in after a moment, so a quick search shows nothing. Under the list the earth begins, almost all white. It is drawn only in light greys, each thing its own shade, up to 35% lighter or darker than one grey. It runs across the whole main area and down to the page's foot, and fills the window when the results are few (`Earth` in `ui/views/jobs.tsx`, drawn on a canvas). A ragged line of ink marks where it begins, and below are sparse grey specks and faint strata. Small things are buried at random across it in the page's ink, some fainter as if deeper, each clear of the others, the words, the shaft and Pip. They include a fish's and a dinosaur's skeletons, a bone, a skull, a shell, a gem, a key, a coin, a worm, a boot, a bottle, stones and one treasure chest. A shaft with a ladder goes down to where Pip, drawn as grey outlines, digs (`PipMiner` with `outline` in `ui/pip.tsx`). Its tail is toward the ladder and its pickaxe, a curved head on a slanting handle, swings toward the page's middle. In the middle, a line in black says these are all the jobs, or that there were none, with the count and any hidden by area. Saved jobs have a tab of their own. Below it the numbers sit on plain cards in the banner's colours, with a pixel outline, pixel pictures and digits, and the best jobs stand as squares on pixel shelves with no sides, a plank under each row, as many as they need; empty places on the last shelf hold dust, a mushroom or a snail with a cobweb under its end, and with no jobs Pip sleeps on the one low shelf; everything else is plain and easy to read, in Rubik, black and white like a social site, with the main buttons, links and marks of where you are in one forest colour: forest green by day, the night forest's turquoise at night. The page you are on has its sidebar icon filled. The sidebar is laid out like a social site's: Pip's head as a one-colour logo (`PipMark`, also the tab icon in orange, `public/icon.svg`), one list of icons and words, and a round button for a new conversation, with the account and a logout button at its foot, narrowing to icons only in smaller windows. Language and theme are on the settings page. The icons are Lucide (ISC licence), and the interface uses no tooltips. What Pip says after an action shows as a small notice at the bottom of the screen. Until the page is ready, with its fonts in, the state loaded and the banner painted, a loading screen in `public/index.html` shows Pip hopping beside a trail of paw prints, for at least a second; `public/boot.js` sets the theme and language before it first shows, and marks the page lite when the browser draws without the graphics card (hardware acceleration off), which stills the jobs page's moving sky and drops its blurs, and `ui.test.ts` checks that its fox still matches `<Pip mood="happy" />`. The login page, `ui/login.tsx`, opens with a pixel art scene from `ui/intro.ts`: leaves cover the screen and part to the sides, the camera pushes into the forest, Pip hops in from the edge of the screen and away into the clearing, turns round, sparks gather into the form, and Pip jumps onto it. The forest is a morning one in the light theme and a night one in the dark, and the theme button switches between them under leaves that close over the whole screen and part again, like at the start. The form stands on a board drawn in the same pixels, wood and parchment in the morning, dark stone at night, and its fields and button take matching colours from \public/styles.css\. It is drawn small in flat colours on two canvases, one behind the form and one in front, and enlarged without smoothing. Pip here is the same sprite as in the app, from `foxPixels` in `ui/pip.tsx`, with a back view for hopping away. A reduced-motion setting goes straight to the form. The theme and language buttons stay in the top left corner in both languages, and a new language flips the board like a sign while Pip jumps off it and back on. The fonts are Fredoka for headings and Rubik for text, and Handjet, a pixel font with Hebrew, on the login, all served from `public/fonts/`.

The language button sets the conversation language too, through the saved settings, so sessions and the daily email follow it.

Nothing on the site has a Save or Cancel button: changes save on their own (`useAutoSave` in `ui/autosave.tsx`). Each save goes a moment after the last change, at once if the page or window is left first, and one at a time, each starting from where the last left off. This covers the settings, the where window, the CV checks, the profile's and rules' text, and the files screen. Nothing shows while saving goes well; only a save that failed says so, in red. Windows close with their ×, Escape or a click outside. Texts that a conversation may change too save from the version they were read at, so that change is never written over. The Gmail account saves once both its boxes are filled and the password's box is left, never while a password is half typed.

For now the site is in Hebrew and the light theme only. `LANGUAGES_ON` and `THEMES_ON` in `ui/app.tsx`, and the same two in `public/boot.js`, are off: on the settings page the whole display section is marked as coming soon, faded, with nothing in it to press, and the login's theme and language buttons are hidden. Setting them to `true` brings them back, with whatever was picked before.

Lists to pick from are drawn by the page (`ui/select.tsx`), not the browser. Each is a box like the text boxes, and a round panel that grows in and fades out, with the choice in use ticked and the slim scrollbar from `ThinBar` in `ui/kit.tsx`. A long list, such as the countries, has a search box that finds a name in Hebrew or English. The jobs page's menus fade out as they close too.

The conversations page (`ui/views/chat.tsx`) is laid out like a chat app's. The open conversation is in the middle, with its messages in one column: yours in a soft bubble, and Pip's as plain text. The tool calls between messages fold into one line of steps, and requests and questions are cards. The list of conversations sits on the page's far side, grouped by day and as wide as the sidebar, and slides in from a button in narrower windows. At the foot is a box to write in, shaped like the jobs search: one pill-shaped row with a + for attaching at its start. At its end are round icon buttons, the access picker (see Security) and, for a new conversation, the model, each opening a list with an icon for every option. The green send button rolls in once there is something to send, and rolls back out when the box is emptied (`useGoSlot` in `ui/kit.tsx`, shared with the search). While Pip works, the same place holds a stop button. A second line or an attachment opens the box up, the text above and the buttons in a row below, until it is empty again. Enter sends. Images, PDFs and text files can be attached to a message by the box's +, by pasting, or by dropping them on the box, five at a time. Images can be PNG, JPG, GIF or WebP, up to 5 MB. PDFs and text files can be up to 20 MB. Each one uploads as it comes in (`uploads.ts`), as base64 inside JSON like every other write. It is kept only when its first bytes match its name, and it is saved in a folder of its own under `web/data/uploads`, which git ignores. Claude sees an image at once. A PDF or text file is named in the message by its place in the project, for Claude to read. The message shows them, and each opens in a new tab. A new conversation starts from a question in the middle, with a few commands as pills. From the sidebar the page opens on a new conversation, or on the one last open since the page loaded. The documents a conversation wrote or names (CVs, letters, reports, the final CV in an application's folder) show at its end, to open or download right there: `/api/sessions/<id>/files` lists them, and `/file?path=…&download=1` saves one.

When a conversation waits for your answer, Pip waits for you in the bottom corner, beside the sidebar, except on the conversations page. It peeks out from behind the sidebar's edge, standing partly past it and leaning out, or up from the bottom edge on a phone, quietly and with no "!". Above it is a speech bubble drawn in pixels like Pip, with a stepped outline and tail. It says only, in the pixel font, that Pip waits for your answer. A click opens that conversation. Pip slides out, leans out a little further every few seconds, and slides back once nothing waits or that conversation is open. The other Pips on the pages, such as the one on the home journey, don't change for it.

## What runs where

- **Live search** runs the portal CLIs directly and uses no Claude usage.
- **Direct edits** use no Claude usage either: the settings, the rules and profile text, the limits and banned wording, the files screen, a CV's or letter's text and rebuild, and an application's status.
- **Everything else** runs a Claude session in this folder, through the Agent SDK with your existing Claude Code login. This is for your own use. Anything shared with other people would need an API key.

## Settings

The settings screen chooses up to 3 markets (any country, an optional city, or remote; at 3 the add row gives way to a note saying to remove one first), the area tiers, work mode, seniority, relocation, roles (picked from a ready-made list by field in `roles.ts`, each searched with the experience level added, or typed in by hand; `/setup` picks them with `bun run web/pick-roles.ts`), salary, fields of interest, how to treat student positions, and the daily run. They are stored in `web/data/settings.json`.

Saving also rewrites the `WEB-SETTINGS` block in `CLAUDE.md` and `search-queries.md`, so `/job-scraper`, `/rank` and `/apply` follow the same choices in new conversations.

Areas match words in a job's location text, from the start of a word. A job listed as "Fürstenfeldbruck, DE" does not match "Bavaria", so list the cities that matter too. Ready-made areas exist for Israel only, in `REGION_PRESETS`.

## Direct edits

`edit.ts` holds them. Saving a document or a CV refuses a file that changed since the page loaded it, so a direct edit never overwrites a session's work. Changing a status follows `/outcome` Step 4: it adds a dated note and, on a row leaving `drafted`, sets the date to today. It does not write `outcome.md`, so use `/outcome` to record what happened in an interview or a rejection.

The limits and banned wording are in `templates/cv/one-page-clean/cv_checks.json`, which `onepage.sty` and `build.py` read at every build.

The files screen edits the commands, agents, knowledge files and CV template. The list in `FILE_GROUPS` is the only way in, so a request cannot reach any other file. `.claude/settings.json` and `policy.ts` can be read there but not saved, and the safety tools and this interface's code are not listed: those keep the approval in a conversation, because they decide what a session may do.

## The daily run

`automation.ts`, switched on in the settings screen and off by default. Once a day from the set hour it searches, ranks, drafts a CV for the best jobs through `/apply`, and emails the CVs to the candidate through `send_mail.py`. It never contacts an employer.

- It runs only while this server is running. If the server starts after the set hour, it runs then, once that day.
- Its sessions use the same `policy.ts` as any other, and anything that would ask for approval is refused instead, so a posting cannot gain more rights through it.
- A job is tried once, even when no CV comes out.
- The Gmail app password is stored in `~/.ai-job-search/mail.json`, outside the project, and the API never returns it. `.claude/settings.json` denies a session reading that folder.

## Security

- **Login:** for now the only account is `admin` with the password `admin`, set in `auth.ts`. The server checks it, and every API call and document needs the session cookie it sets. Sessions live in memory, so starting the server again asks for the login again.
- The server listens on this computer only. It checks the Host and Origin headers and requires a cookie, so other websites cannot reach it.
- `policy.ts` decides what a session may do without asking, by the access picked for each conversation in the chat:
  - **Ask me** (the default): reading the project, research, the commands in `.claude/settings.json`, known LaTeX compiles and writing workflow output run on their own, and everything else waits for your approval in the browser.
  - **Automatic in the project**: also every edit inside the project, except the files that change what the agent may do (`.claude/settings`, commands, agents, `web/`, `CLAUDE.md`, `CV_RULES.md`, the CV checks, any `SKILL.md`). Commands, deletes and anything outside the project still ask.
  - **Full access**: everything runs without asking. Postings are untrusted text, so a pasted posting could then make the agent do anything; the chat warns while it is picked.
  - The deny rules in `.claude/settings.json` hold in every mode, and a daily run always uses Ask me, refusing whatever would ask. Switching a conversation to a more open access lets a request that is already waiting go ahead if the new access allows it.
- Conversations and the access token are stored in `web/data/`, which git ignores.
