# Web interface

A local interface for everything in this workspace. Double-click `start-web.cmd` in the repository root. It opens at `http://127.0.0.1:4174`, and the window must stay open while you use it.

## How it is built

The interface is React, written in `ui/`. The server bundles it into `public/build/` on every start, so there is no separate build step. Text is in `ui/i18n.ts`, in Hebrew and English, and `ui.test.ts` checks that both have the same keys. Pip, the companion, is an abstract fox in big pixel blocks in `ui/pip.tsx`, and the home screen's banner is the login's forest at rest, drawn by `paintStill` in `ui/intro.ts`. The banner is the one part of the app in pixel art, with a board and the Handjet pixel font like the login's, and at its foot the forest dissolves into the page in a dither of pixel blocks. Along the journey at its foot Pip stands on the sign of the stage you are at; the browser remembers the stage it was last seen on, so a stage reached while you were elsewhere shows as Pip hopping on to it, a stage at a time. The jobs page is a single search box over a moving forest-rainbow glow, with the first few of your roles in a straight row of liquid-glass buttons and a + that unfolds the rest downward in a curved fan like a Dock stack. The labels open one after another and fold back in reverse order, curving inward in either language. The fan never scrolls and keeps to one column: it holds only what fits above the window's bottom, and when more are left, its last place says how many and opens a list of every role, in a menu like the search settings'. It closes on selection, an outside click, Escape or tabbing away. Arrow keys, Home and End move through the roles; reduced motion skips the animation. At the box's end, the search settings, with their name floating over them under the pointer as the sparkles' does (where, which opens a window to change the markets and areas right there, each area's rating a dropdown in the rating's own colour, and the search's options as rows of round choices, kept with the settings as `quickSearch`) and a colour-turning sparkles button for the two searches that use Claude, and once you type, a search button flies in. When the first search of a visit starts, the box and the roles rise to the top; Pip rides up on the box for a moment, frightened and shivering, then leaps down to the bottom of the window and runs off its edge, and the results rise in one after another, as a plain list like a search engine's: each with the company's initial on a round tile in a forest colour, the title, company and place, chips for how long ago it was posted and its area, and round buttons to open the posting and to check it and apply. Under the pointer, or reached by the keyboard, a button's name floats over it a moment later, in a small white pill like the menus, which the page draws (not the browser's tooltip). While the search runs, the middle of the page shows only Pip's face, the logo, in grey, breathing, inside a thin turning ring. It has no words, just a label for screen readers, and fades in after a moment, so a quick search shows nothing. Under the list the earth begins, almost all white. It is drawn only in light greys, each thing its own shade, up to 35% lighter or darker than one grey. It runs across the whole main area and down to the page's foot, and fills the window when the results are few (`Earth` in `ui/views/jobs.tsx`, drawn on a canvas). A ragged line of ink marks where it begins, and below are sparse grey specks and faint strata. Small things are buried at random across it in the page's ink, some fainter as if deeper, each clear of the others, the words, the shaft and Pip. They include a fish's and a dinosaur's skeletons, a bone, a skull, a shell, a gem, a key, a coin, a worm, a boot, a bottle, stones and one treasure chest. A shaft with a ladder goes down to where Pip, drawn as grey outlines, digs (`PipMiner` with `outline` in `ui/pip.tsx`). Its tail is toward the ladder and its pickaxe, a curved head on a slanting handle, swings toward the page's middle. In the middle, a line in black says these are all the jobs, or that there were none, with the count and any hidden by area. Saved jobs have a tab of their own. Below it the numbers sit on plain cards in the banner's colours, with a pixel outline, pixel pictures and digits, and the best jobs stand as squares on pixel shelves with no sides, a plank under each row, as many as they need; empty places on the last shelf hold dust, a mushroom or a snail with a cobweb under its end, and with no jobs Pip sleeps on the one low shelf; everything else is plain and easy to read, in Rubik, black and white like a social site, with the main buttons, links and marks of where you are in one forest colour: forest green by day, the night forest's turquoise at night. The page you are on has its sidebar icon filled. The sidebar is laid out like a social site's: Pip's face as a one-colour logo (`PipMark`: his face alone from his icon's blocks, the eyes and the nose cut out, with a small jaw), one list of icons and words, and the main button for a new conversation in the AI's colours (`AiNewChat`, below), with the account and a logout button at its foot, narrowing to icons only in smaller windows. Language and theme are on the settings page. The icons are Lucide (ISC licence), and the interface uses no tooltips. What Pip says after an action, he says peeking out from behind the sidebar, in his speech bubble, for a few seconds; so does he when he updates himself, while he is away and once he is back. Until the page is ready, with its fonts in, the state loaded and the banner painted, a loading screen in `public/index.html` shows Pip hopping beside a trail of paw prints, for at least a second; `public/boot.js` sets the theme and language before it first shows, and marks the page lite when the browser draws without the graphics card (hardware acceleration off), which stills the jobs page's moving sky and drops its blurs, and `ui.test.ts` checks that its fox still matches `<Pip mood="happy" />`. The login page, `ui/login.tsx`, opens with a pixel art scene from `ui/intro.ts`: leaves cover the screen and part to the sides, the camera pushes into the forest, Pip hops in from the edge of the screen and away into the clearing, turns round, sparks gather into the form, and Pip jumps onto it. The forest is a morning one in the light theme and a night one in the dark, and the theme button switches between them under leaves that close over the whole screen and part again, like at the start. The form stands on a board drawn in the same pixels, wood and parchment in the morning, dark stone at night, and its fields and button take matching colours from \public/styles.css\. It is drawn small in flat colours on two canvases, one behind the form and one in front, and enlarged without smoothing. Pip here is the same sprite as in the app, from `foxPixels` in `ui/pip.tsx`, with a back view for hopping away. Pip's icon is that fox too, standing, in his blocks (`ICON` in `ui/pip.tsx`): `icon.ts` draws it at each size, as the tab's `public/icon.ico` (`bun icon.ts` writes it again after a change to him) and as the installer's and the program's icons. A reduced-motion setting goes straight to the form. The theme and language buttons stay in the top left corner in both languages, and a new language flips the board like a sign while Pip jumps off it and back on. The fonts are Fredoka for headings and Rubik for text, and Handjet, a pixel font with Hebrew, on the login, all served from `public/fonts/`.

The language button sets the conversation language too, through the saved settings, so sessions and the daily email follow it.

Nothing on the site has a Save or Cancel button: changes save on their own (`useAutoSave` in `ui/autosave.tsx`). Each save goes a moment after the last change, at once if the page or window is left first, and one at a time, each starting from where the last left off. This covers the settings, the where window, the CV checks, the profile's and rules' text, and the files screen. Nothing shows while saving goes well; only a save that failed says so, in red. Windows close with their ×, Escape or a click outside. Texts that a conversation may change too save from the version they were read at, so that change is never written over. The Gmail account saves once both its boxes are filled and the password's box is left, never while a password is half typed.

For now the site is in Hebrew and the light theme only. `LANGUAGES_ON` and `THEMES_ON` in `ui/app.tsx`, and the same two in `public/boot.js`, are off: on the settings page the whole display section is marked as coming soon, faded, with nothing in it to press, and the login's theme and language buttons are hidden. Setting them to `true` brings them back, with whatever was picked before.

Lists to pick from are drawn by the page (`ui/select.tsx`), not the browser. Each is a box like the text boxes, and a round panel that grows in and fades out, with the choice in use ticked and the slim scrollbar from `ThinBar` in `ui/kit.tsx`. A long list, such as the countries, has a search box that finds a name in Hebrew or English. The jobs page's menus fade out as they close too.

The conversations page (`ui/views/chat.tsx`) is laid out like a chat app's. The open conversation is in the middle, with its messages in one column: yours in a soft bubble, and Pip's as plain text. The tool calls between messages fold into one line of steps, and requests and questions are cards. A run of questions to prepare, each with talking points on the lines under it as `/interview` writes them, shows as cards that turn over; questions Pip asks the user, each with why he asks on the same line, stay text. An answer in Hebrew keeps every paragraph and list with a Hebrew word in it on the right, a list of sources whose items start with a link too (`Markdown` in `ui/kit.tsx`, given the answer's language). The AI's moving colours (`AiSky`) speed up while a button that holds them is hovered or has keyboard focus, rather than growing brighter; the rate is set once, each cloud going on from where it is (eased in by setting it on every frame, it held back every other change the hover made on the same button for a third of a second in newer Chromes, Brave 157 among them). The list of conversations has no names floating over its buttons, its icons say enough. The sidebar's button for a new conversation, across it (`AiNewChat` in `ui/kit.tsx`; a round plus on the clouds alone where the sidebar keeps only its icons), is a pill whose ground is the AI's own, as behind the microphone (AiSky, its clouds in the microphone's proportions, small over the sky's blue); over it lies a pill of the page's own colour a little in from the rim, its edge rippling in a few long soft waves that run slowly round it (a SMIL animation of a smooth outline), so at rest the clouds show as a wavy ring; on hover they quicken and the pill shrinks away gently, in about 0.4s, its waves still running, until the clouds fill the button and the words turn white as the pill leaves them, and off it the pill grows back at once but softly, over 0.55s, and the words go dark at once; its words never wrap. The list's head has the search's round button and the one that hides the list; the search opens out of its button into a pill across the head, the way back at its end, closes back into it, and marks what it found in each title. Under the head are rows of places, as ChatGPT's: a new conversation, the daily run's conversations with when it last ran at its end, and the files and the tools; while the list opens what is in it keeps its width and is uncovered rather than squeezed. Projects begin with a "new project" row, in the same ink as the rest, each with its folder and its count in a soft pill; the slim scrollbar stands out at the list's outer edge, past the conversations. Under a new conversation's box the starts are rows with icons, as ChatGPT's suggestions, and the model and its thinking are one quiet button of words in the box ("Opus · גבוהה", `ModelMenu`) opening a panel under it: the models as rows, and the level of thinking as a slider (`EffortSlider`), a thick pill like a phone's volume slider over four levels, filled to the level with a soft rainbow of the AI's colours flowing along it, quicker the higher the level, the level's name inside it, to press or drag. It stands on the level the user's own Claude Code settings give the chosen model (`claudeEfforts` in `/api/state`, read by `defaultEfforts` in `agent.ts`), says so, and offers the way back to it. The box asks "לשאול את פיפ כל דבר". The top buttons' names grow inward from the page's edge, so one never pushes the page aside. The chat has themes (`ui/chat-themes.ts`, `ui/chat-theme.tsx`), picked from a round palette button at its top: a gallery of cards, each with its scene moving, its name and whether it is light or dark. Each is an original pixel-art scene from Pip's world painted behind the chat, with the chat's colours to match (styles.css `.chat[data-chat-theme]`, for the chat alone): a morning meadow, a rainy day, a sunset, the forest at night with fireflies, snow, a starry night with Pip on a hill looking up, and Santa Pip: a Christmas night, Pip in Santa's hat (drawn over his own sprite, `SANTA_HAT`) in front of a tree hung with blinking lights and a star, presents under it, little houses lit on the hill, snow, now and then a wave from Pip and a sleigh crossing the sky, all in the margin beside the conversation's card; "plain" is the chat as it was, and the default. A scene is painted at a few screen pixels to each of its own, what stays still once for each size and what moves worked out from the time alone, at 15 frames a second; with reduced motion it is a still. The words lie on the scene as they are, no box under them: the scene keeps quiet where they go, its specks (snow, rain, stars, fireflies) faint in the conversation's column (`runScene`'s quiet, a gradient across the moving layer) and its land low, behind the message box, while what is someone (Pip, the tree's lights, the sleigh) is drawn over all of it as it is. The pick is kept in this browser. How the agent writes for this page is said to it after Claude Code's own instructions (`chat-style.ts`, the systemPrompt's append, and Codex's developer instructions): the conversation's language with no maqaf or dash between words, its steps and helpers described in that language, no paths, instruction files, tool names or step numbers, documents named rather than linked (they show as cards), and only the formatting the page draws. Saved answers had a path in 127 of them, a maqaf in 116 and an instruction file or tool by name in about 110. The page draws the rest when it comes anyway: a sub-list under its item, a numbered list from its own first number, boxes as marks, five and six hashes as headings, and, in a chat answer, a path or a link to a file as the file's name (a document opening, `/file`), a maqaf as a space and a dash between words as a comma. A step refused in an unattended run says so in the step, not as a red line for each. Each conversation's agent gets a scratch folder of its own in `SCRATCHPAD` (under the temporary folder), as the workflows' commands write `"${SCRATCHPAD:?...}/page.html"`, and the policy lets that one variable through; before, every such command was refused in the daily run and asked about in a conversation. The list of conversations sits on the page's far side, grouped by day and as wide as the sidebar, and slides in from a button in narrower windows. At the foot is a box to write in, shaped like the jobs search: one pill-shaped row with a + for attaching at its start. At its end are round icon buttons, the access picker (see Security) and, for a new conversation, the model and how hard Claude thinks (the SDK's effort: low, medium, high or very high, which is `xhigh`, or the user's own default), each opening a list with an icon for every option. The effort a conversation started with stays with it, and goes to Claude when it takes over after a quick application. The green send button rolls in once there is something to send, and rolls back out when the box is emptied (`useGoSlot` in `ui/kit.tsx`, shared with the search). While Pip works, the same place holds a stop button. A second line or an attachment opens the box up, the text above and the buttons in a row below, until it is empty again. Enter sends. Images, PDFs and text files can be attached to a message by the box's +, by pasting, or by dropping them on the box, five at a time. Images can be PNG, JPG, GIF or WebP, up to 5 MB. PDFs and text files can be up to 20 MB. Each one uploads as it comes in (`uploads.ts`), as base64 inside JSON like every other write. It is kept only when its first bytes match its name, and it is saved in a folder of its own under `user/app/uploads`, which git ignores. Claude sees an image at once. A PDF or text file is named in the message by its place in the project, for Claude to read. The message shows them, and each opens in a new tab. A new conversation starts from a question in the middle, with a few commands as pills. From the sidebar the page opens on a new conversation, or on the one last open since the page loaded. The documents a conversation wrote or names (CVs, letters, reports, the final CV in an application's folder) show at its end, to open or download right there: `/api/sessions/<id>/files` lists them, and `/file?path=…&download=1` saves one.

When a conversation waits for your answer, Pip waits for you in the bottom corner, beside the sidebar, except on the conversations page. It peeks out from behind the sidebar's edge, standing partly past it and leaning out, or up from the bottom edge on a phone, quietly and with no "!". Above it is a speech bubble drawn in pixels like Pip, with a stepped outline and tail. It says only, in the pixel font, that Pip waits for your answer. A click opens that conversation. Pip slides out, leans out a little further every few seconds, and slides back once nothing waits or that conversation is open. The other Pips on the pages, such as the one on the home journey, don't change for it.

### Which AI

The settings' "AI connection" card picks the primary AI, Claude or ChatGPT (`ai.ts`), and shows whether each is connected. Claude is connected through this computer's Claude Code login, with nothing to set. ChatGPT connects the same way, signed in with your account through Codex, installed in the project at a fixed version (`@openai/codex` in `package.json`; `clis.ts`), so its use comes out of your ChatGPT plan's allowance rather than being paid per use. The card's "Sign in with your account" button opens Codex's own sign-in in a window of its own on this computer; the login stays in Codex's own folder (`~/.codex`), and the card sees it within a few seconds. A plain answer is one run of Codex in an empty folder of its own under the temp directory, read-only (`codex exec -s read-only --ephemeral --ignore-user-config`), with what was attached copied in; nothing of the project is there. There is no API key: ChatGPT works only through the account (the key option was removed on 2026-11-09), and a key an earlier version saved is deleted, unread, when the server starts (`forgetKeys` in `chatbots.ts`). Nothing else asks which AI to use. Work in the project goes to the agent `pickAgent` in `agent.ts` picks: with ChatGPT primary, ChatGPT's own agent (Codex), never Claude in its place, and when its account isn't signed in the conversation says so with a button to sign in (`no_agent`); with Claude primary, Claude, or ChatGPT's agent while Claude isn't signed in. Each picks on a sign-in asked afresh when the last answer said it was missing (`cliStatus(true)`). A sign-in its server refuses (an expired login that `auth status` still reports as signed in) counts as signed out until signed in again (`loginRefused` in `clis.ts`), and the conversation offers that sign-in; a conversation whose AI never got to work starts again from its first message on the next one. When the primary fails on a quota or an outage, the other takes over if it is connected, with a line in the conversation saying so. In a run of many steps the one that failed isn't tried again. Gemini was tried and removed: Google no longer lets its CLI sign in with a personal account, and its terms forbid reaching a personal account through other software.

- **The ranking and the smart search** are done by the server with that AI, so the menu's two jobs and their buttons elsewhere (home, the new conversation's starts, the tools page) work with any of them. `/rank` or `/job-scraper` with more after it, typed in a conversation, is still Claude's command.
  - Ranking (`ranker.ts`) runs `/rank`'s own tools: `rank_state.py` picks up to ten new jobs and writes the scores back, the portal's `detail` command or `fetch_page.py` gets each posting, and `posting_guard.py` cleans it. The model only reads one cleaned posting at a time against the scoring framework's gates and dimensions, the profile and the search settings, and answers with its scores as JSON, which is checked before it is written. It has no tools, so a posting written to steer it can at most bend its own score. Claude gets no tools either here (`tools: []`, nothing from the project's settings). The profile goes without the phone, email and profile links, and the settings without the salary. One thing `/rank` does that this doesn't is search for the employer's own copy of a posting that won't open; such a job is marked unreachable and tried again on the next run.
  - The smart search (`scraper.ts`) searches every role in the settings in every market on each portal, with the live search's own code, over the last 14 days. `scrape_state.py candidates` leaves out what the list already has, the model gives each new hit a quick fit (high, medium, low) from its title, company, place and area and says which aren't worth keeping, and `scrape_state.py store` saves them all, the ones not kept as skipped. A hit the model didn't judge isn't stored, so it is found again next time. The deadline is left for the ranking to find.
  - Applying (`applier.ts`) is the server's own, with whichever AI is first: `/apply` as an agent read some 260,000 characters of instructions and then took 30 to 120 steps, each sending the whole conversation again, with two helper agents besides, and a few applications used up a plan's usage. Now the posting is opened and cleaned with no AI (as above, or pasted text by `posting_guard.py`), its source host sorted as `/apply` does, and the model asked four times or so: the fit, judged by the framework (a location or language FAIL stops it before any CV); a CV written from the profile in the template's own commands; a second pass that checks every fact against the profile and the master CV and gives the wording the humanizer; and a fix each time `build.py` stops, up to four. The calls after the first begin with the same instructions, so the model's cache serves them. A source using any LaTeX command the template doesn't have (`\directlua`, `\input`...) is sent back before it is built, so a posting can't use the CV to read a file into the page. Then Step 6b: the tracker row (`recordDraft` in `edit.ts`), the posting archived with the guard's counts, the ATS score, and what the CV adds beyond the profile, which its first line names as a comment. The daily run's is strict: a CV only for a strong or good fit, and none for a posting the guard flags. `/apply --full` is the agent, Claude's or ChatGPT's in Codex, for when company research, a reviewer, a look at the PDF or a cover letter is wanted; so is `/apply` with files attached. A link sent alone in a new conversation is an application too, decided on the server, and its bubble shows the link as it was sent, with no command before it (from a job's page as well). A company or role the model gives only in Hebrew no longer stops it: `job` stands in for the company in the folder's name, and the day for the role. Once the fit is judged, the conversation is called by the role and company. The report names no commands: a cover letter, company research or changes are asked for in the same conversation.
  - Each run shows as a conversation: its steps, then the report (best fits, why, closing soon, left out, couldn't open; or the new jobs by fit). A message written there afterwards goes to the agent that works in the project (Claude's, or ChatGPT's in Codex), which gets everything said and done so far ahead of it (`briefing` in `agent.ts`), so asking for the CV, a letter or a change after a quick application does it there, in the same conversation; only with no agent that can work here is it answered as a plain conversation, as before. A saved conversation of that kind is known by its steps, so one from before this goes to the agent too. When Claude can't go on in a conversation (its usage limit, its sign-in refused, any failure) and ChatGPT's account is signed in, ChatGPT's agent in Codex takes over there, commands too, told everything said and done before the message and the steps Claude had run for it (`toCodex` in `agent.ts`); a plain chat is left only when no agent can work, and a conversation that went to one goes back to an agent with its next message. Claude's own limit message shows as a line in the user's language with when it resets, and the plain chat no longer sends the user to a command. Signing in from a conversation works on Windows now: the window was opened with `start Claude <exe> auth login`, and start takes an unquoted first word for the program to run, so the sign-in never ran and a refused one stayed refused however often it was opened. The title goes in quotes now (`windowsLoginLine` in `clis.ts`), cmd waits for the window, and only once it closes does the sign-in count again; then the newest conversation that waited on it starts again by itself (`onSignedIn`, `retryAfterSignIn` in `agent.ts`), the introduction's reading of the documents included. The sign-in block says what happened in a calm line, its way in is a borderless pill, and the run's "ended with an error" under it is left out. A message that is a command shows as the command's name, without its options. One run at a time.
- **ChatGPT as a whole agent**: with ChatGPT primary and its account signed in, every conversation, commands included, is ChatGPT working in the project as Claude does, through Codex's app-server (`codex.ts`): one thread per conversation in the project folder, following `AGENTS.md`, which points it to the same `user/CLAUDE.md` and `.claude/` workflows, with instructions on using its own tools where those name Claude's. Each command it would run (unwrapped from the PowerShell call it comes in) and each file it would change comes to the session first and is judged as Claude's are: `.claude/settings.json`'s deny rules (checked here, in any part of a chained command, plus anything naming a folder of secrets), then the conversation's access (`policy.ts`), then the approval card; questions it asks come up as Claude's do. Codex runs plain reads on its own, without asking, so the secrets on this computer are sealed with Windows' DPAPI (`vault.ts`): the mail password is stored as ciphertext, opened only by the server, and a file saved before sealing is sealed the first time it is read. Writes stay inside the project (`workspace-write`), and the network is open, since every command that isn't a plain read is judged first; web search is live, as Claude's is. Where a workflow hands work to one of `.claude/agents/` (`/apply`'s reviewer, `/rank`'s scorers), Codex starts a sub-agent of its own, a thread of its own on the same connection: its steps show nested under the line that started it, its end is not the conversation's, and any file change it asks for is declined, since those agents only read (they hold untrusted posting text). Where a workflow reads a PDF, `tools/verify_pdf.py --render` writes its pages as pictures in a `pages/` folder beside it (only for a PDF under `user/cv/` or `user/cover_letters/`, the places its pre-approval covers), and Codex looks at them. So applying is `/apply` here too, as Claude's is; the ranking and the smart search keep their server versions.
- **Conversations**: with ChatGPT primary every conversation is ChatGPT's agent (above). With Claude primary, when Claude fails on a plain message (not a command, and no tool run yet), the conversation goes on as a plain one with ChatGPT if its account is signed in (`chatbots.ts`, `CHAT_SYSTEM`): chat only, with no access or model picker, each answer one run of Codex that sees only the conversation.
- **The daily run** searches with no AI at all (`scraper.ts searchDaily`: LinkedIn and freehire for every role, and the Telegram channels read by `telegram.ts`), checks the portals with no AI either (each hit judged by `judgeByRules`: a senior title on a junior search, an excluded area, a student position the settings exclude, or a title outside the roles' field), and applies the same quick way, with whichever AI is first. The sites the job-scraper skill searched with WebSearch need a search engine, so they are left to that skill in a conversation.

## What runs where

- **Live search** runs the portal CLIs directly and uses no Claude usage.
- **Direct edits** use no Claude usage either: the settings, the rules and profile text, the limits and banned wording, the files screen, a CV's or letter's text and rebuild, and an application's status.
- **Everything else** runs a Claude session in this folder, through the Agent SDK with your existing Claude Code login. This is for your own use. Anything shared with other people would need an API key.
- **ChatGPT**, as the primary AI or taking over from it, uses no Claude usage. It goes to OpenAI on your own account there, signed in through Codex.

## Settings

The settings screen chooses up to 3 markets (any country, an optional city, or remote; at 3 the add row gives way to a note saying to remove one first), the area tiers, work mode, seniority, relocation, roles (picked from a ready-made list by field in `roles.ts`, each searched with the experience level added, or typed in by hand; `/setup` picks them with `bun run web/pick-roles.ts`), salary, fields of interest, how to treat student positions, and the daily run. They are stored in `user/app/settings.json`.

Saving also rewrites the `WEB-SETTINGS` block in `user/CLAUDE.md` and `user/search-queries.md`, so `/job-scraper`, `/rank` and `/apply` follow the same choices in new conversations.

Areas match words in a job's location text, from the start of a word. A job listed as "Fürstenfeldbruck, DE" does not match "Bavaria", so list the cities that matter too. Ready-made areas exist for Israel only, in `REGION_PRESETS`.

## Direct edits

`edit.ts` holds them. Saving a document or a CV refuses a file that changed since the page loaded it, so a direct edit never overwrites a session's work. Changing a status follows `/outcome` Step 4: it adds a dated note and, on a row leaving `drafted`, sets the date to today. It does not write `outcome.md`, so use `/outcome` to record what happened in an interview or a rejection.

The limits and banned wording are in `user/cv_checks.json`, which `onepage.sty` and `build.py` read at every build.

The files screen edits the commands, agents, knowledge files and CV template. The list in `FILE_GROUPS` is the only way in, so a request cannot reach any other file. `.claude/settings.json` and `policy.ts` can be read there but not saved, and the safety tools and this interface's code are not listed: those keep the approval in a conversation, because they decide what a session may do.

## The daily run

`automation.ts`, switched on in the settings screen and off by default. Once a day from the set hour it searches and checks the portals with no AI (above), and its conversations wait in a drawer of their own at the foot of the conversations' list, not among the user's (`Session.auto`). It reads the new jobs' postings with no AI so that each has its automatic score (`match.ts`), drafts a CV for the best jobs the quick way (`applier.ts`, above), and emails the CVs to the candidate through `send_mail.py`. It ranks nothing with the AI: a job's AI score is there only when the user asked for it, and `/apply`'s own evaluation still stops a poor fit before any CV. It never contacts an employer.

- It runs only while this server is running. If the server starts after the set hour, it runs then, once that day.
- A job is picked by its AI score when it has one, and otherwise by its automatic score, once its posting is read (a title alone is too little to draft for). The settings' lowest score is on the AI's scale; an automatic score, a keyword match that runs higher, needs 15 more (`AUTO_LIFT`), so 75 by default. The picks go by how far each clears its own bar.
- Its sessions use the same `policy.ts` as any other, and anything that would ask for approval is refused instead, so a posting cannot gain more rights through it.
- A job is tried once, even when no CV comes out.
- The Gmail app password is stored in `~/.ai-job-search/mail.json`, outside the project, and the API never returns it. `.claude/settings.json` denies a session reading that folder.

## Live alerts

Turned on in the settings, the server watches the search with no AI and tells each new job the moment it is stored (`alerts.ts`, wired in `server.ts`), so the user can apply first.

- Two lanes, each at its own pace, so the slow one never holds the quick one back. The quick search (LinkedIn and freehire one day back, `searchDaily` with `days: "1"`, and the Telegram channels), about ten seconds, runs every 5, 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes. LinkedIn is asked for the newest first (the tool's `--sort recent`, `sortBy=DD`): it gives one page of ten, and by relevance a search two weeks back had none of the day's jobs on it.
- The sites' sweep (`scanPortals`) runs every half hour: an employer on its own site or a recruiting platform is read each time (`fresh` 25 minutes), one read through AllJobs's or Drushim's page hourly (some 400 on two sites, read a page at a time, most of the sweep's quarter of an hour), one whose last check found nothing rests six hours, and the Israeli boards and the recruiting platforms' new boards are searched each time. What it reads is stored and told a minute's worth at a time, not once all is read; a batch whose storing fails doesn't stop the others, and the checks are written before the failure is reported.
- Both run quietly, with no conversation for them; a lane starts only while the daily run or a search of the user's own isn't going, and never twice at once.
- The jobs come from the scraper as it stores them (`found` in `judgeAndStore`): only jobs new to the list and kept by the rules, each looked at once. The rules (`judgeByRules`) put a title in the roles' field by a good share of a role's own words, or by a field word that stands on its own (`fieldTest` in `roles.ts`): not "engineer", "system", "quality", "development" or "economist" alone, nor one word of a role's title ("machine" of machine learning), and "AI", "cloud" or "data" only beside a word for who does the work (an AI engineer, not an AI video creator). A job with no place given is judged by a place its title names, and a place's dashes are spaces ("Kiryat-Gat").
- Of those, only what was just published is told (`justPublished`): a job whose source gives its day when published today, or yesterday in the night's first two hours; a job with no day, from an employer's own site, when the site was read with no error before the round, and not when one read brings more than five at once (a site read in a new way, or a list whose links changed). The rest go into the list with no alert, since a search two weeks back and a site read for the first time are full of older jobs.
- Before a job is told, its posting is read as the list reads it (`worthTelling` in `alerts.ts`, `readNow` in `postings.ts`), LinkedIn's too, the few of a round: a job the list would leave out isn't told, a junior search's job asking for three years or more or with a senior title, or one found closed; nor a student's position when the settings rank those low. A page that won't open says nothing against the job, and what isn't read within three minutes is judged by its title.
- Each job's group (`groups.ts`): **high-tech and known**, an employer on the portals page (by the portal it was read for, or its name without words like Ltd, Systems or בע"מ), one found on the recruiting platforms, or a job on a platform tech companies hire through (Comeet, Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable and others); or **low-tech and unknown**, anything else. The job list has the same split as a filter.
- An alert is a notice on the computer (Windows' toast through PowerShell, a Mac's through osascript, `notify-send` elsewhere), its words handed over in the environment or as arguments and escaped there, never written into the command, a click opening the job or the job list; and an email to the daily run's address through its Gmail account. Both list the high-tech and known jobs first. The settings' test button sends one of each.
## Employers' own sites first

An employer that publishes its jobs on its own careers site is read there, and a job board that also lists it only when its site doesn't answer or shows no jobs at all (`Portal.direct` in `portals.ts`, `readEmployer` in `boards.ts`). The sites were found and checked on 2026-11-14, for the 155 employers read on AllJobs that had jobs in the roles' fields; those whose robots.txt refuses to be read, or that publish no list of their own, stay on AllJobs.

- **A list in the page's own markup** (`{ links }`): the page, what a job's link looks like there, and where a title sits when its link only says "read more". `readLinks` takes a title from the link's words, a share-by-email link's subject, the nearest heading, or the link's own words, and follows the next pages (`pages`) or steps through them (`step`, SAP SuccessFactors' `startrow`). The career sites of SAP SuccessFactors, AdamTotal and TopMatch are read this way, from the list they render.
- **A careers app that answers only a POST**: Redmatch (`{ redmatch }`, Clalit's) and Workday (`{ workday }`, Intel's, Flex's, GE Vernova's). Sean allowed the server to send these on 2026-11-14 (`jsonPoster` in `boards.ts`): only to addresses written in `portals.ts`, never to one a posting gives, robots.txt asked first by the tool `fetch_page.py` uses, https only, no redirects, JSON out and at most 10 MB back within 45 seconds, one request at a time to a site. A Redmatch job's own page is read from the same service (`fetchPosting` in `ranker.ts`).
- **A list a site draws in the browser**, read from where the site itself takes it (`{ own }` in `DIRECT`, a reader each in `boards.ts`):
  - **Israel Aerospace Industries**: one file of all its jobs, which its page names (`readIai`).
  - **Elbit Systems**: one file of all its open jobs, `/cron/jobs.json` (`parseElbit`).
  - **Bezeq**: its own service's list, read with a GET (`parseBezeq`).
  - **Menora Mivtachim**, its pension company's too: the list in its Next.js page's own data (`parseMenora`).
  - **The Phoenix**: the list in its Nuxt page's state, whose short names are resolved by reading the values the page passes, nothing of it run (`nuxtState`, `parsePhoenix`).
  - Elbit's job page draws the job in the browser, and Bezeq's keeps its words hidden until a click, which the posting guard drops, so a job of theirs is read from the same list (`listedJob` in `posting-read.ts`), and a job the list no longer has is closed.
  - A job page with no data of its own for the job (no JobPosting long enough: Jobify's stops at 200 characters) is read for its own part that holds the job, not whole (`mainContent` in `posting-read.ts`): the nav, header, footer and aside, hidden elements (by style, attribute, or Bootstrap's and Tailwind's hiding classes), and blocks named as menus, banners, cookie notices, sharing, related jobs or the site's own offers go, unless one holds half the page's words; then from the body down into the box that holds four fifths of its words outside links, never into a paragraph or a list, and inside it blocks mostly of links go. Checked on AllJobs, Matrix, Dialog, Jobify, Aman, Elad, Amazon, JobMaster, Mobileye, GotFriends and Google; a page with no such part is read whole as before. A posting kept whole by the older reader is read again when its job is opened (`PAGE_READER` in `postings.ts`), its old words kept when the page has no such part any more.
- **A careers page the user adds** on the portals page that embeds Comeet, Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby or Workable is read on that platform (`embeddedBoard`).
- **A job's link the user sends in the chat** (a new conversation, a message, or /apply from a job's page) on Comeet, Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby or Workable: its board is read at once in the background, and the company joins the found employers when it has a job in Israel, as one the daily run came upon would (`learnFromLinks` in `discover.ts`, at most three boards a message). Both readers add to `found-employers.json` as it is when their reads end, so neither writes over the other. Comeet's board pages stopped naming the company in their title in November 2026 (Spark Hire Recruit): the name is read from the page's data or its og:title, and a company kept under its board's address is read again once a day until its name is there.
- **A list written out in full** (`{ text }`), its jobs with no page of their own, to apply by email or a form (FIBI and Mataf, Bank of Jerusalem, Kan, IDI and some 30 more): each job is the block where the site's pattern finds its title (named groups `title`, `id`, `place`, `textBlocks`), known by the list's address with `pip-job=<its id, or a hash of its title>`, as the job list keys a job by its address without its #. The posting reader reads that block alone (`textPosting`), never the whole list, and a job the list no longer has is closed. A list may run over several pages (`more`), and a job whose page its id makes (`link`, Leumi's `/he/node/{id}`, Tel-Hai's and Freesbe's) is known by that page.
- **The civil service** (`{ gov }`): the jobs site of the civil service fills in from one public list of all its open tenders (`GOV_FEED`, a GET of some 2 MB), each with its office's number; a government body's jobs are its offices'. It is read once for all of them in a run. Its hospitals hire outside its tenders too, so they stay on their boards. The Ministry of Defense has its own site and list (`MOD_JOBS`).
- **Other services of an employer's own**: a WordPress site's type of jobs (`{ wp }`, `wp-json/wp/v2/<type>`) or its feed (`{ feed }`); a Jibe site's search (`{ jibe }`, Schneider Electric); Topmatch's Redmatch boards, as Clalit's (Bar-Ilan, Analyst, Tadiran and others), and HOT's service, which answer only a POST.
- `readLinks` reads a link relative to the page's `<base href>` (the municipalities' sites), a job's page from its share buttons (WhatsApp, Facebook, LinkedIn, X, Telegram) when it is on the list's own site, a title inside a card that is all one link, and one page however its escapes are cased.
- The survey of 2026-11-14 covered the 263 other employers read on a board: some 130 are now read on their own sites. The rest stay on their boards: their robots.txt refuses or can't be confirmed, a bot check or a login stands in front of the list, the list needs a form posted to WordPress (Shikun & Binui, Haifa Port, Hunter's boards), the list is a global one with no filter for Israel (DHL, Netafim, Siemens Energy), or there is no list of their own.
- Still read on AllJobs, and why: Rafael answers a bot check; Clal's service is under a path its robots.txt refuses; Cellcom is behind Incapsula and Maccabi's search behind reCAPTCHA; Partner's careers address shows "page not found"; Fattal's list gives no job a page of its own. A site's protection is never worked around.
- **Job boards and agencies** are read rather than searched on the web, each the way it publishes its list, with the roles' fields picking the lists:
  - **Gotfriends**: its categories for the roles' fields and its graduates', two pages of ten each (`readGotfriends`), its jobs unnamed, as the agency lists them.
  - **Nisha**: a page per role, two pages each (`readNisha`).
  - **Jobnet**: the first page of each profession, fifteen jobs marked up as schema.org JobPostings with their employers (`readJobnet`). Its next pages are a form posted back, so they aren't read; a daily run keeps up with the first.
  - **Nefesh B'Nefesh's job board**: its WordPress job feed of the technical categories, one request (`parseJobFeed`).
  - **Ethosia**: its jobs through its WordPress site's own list (`wp-json/wp/v2/job`), only the starting levels for a junior search, and only those of the last four months (`readEthosia`).
  - Not read: Secret Tel Aviv's robots.txt refuses it; Jobify shows its jobs only to a signed-in user; HireMe's API is refused by its robots.txt, and its sitemap lists 20,000 Israeli jobs with no titles, gathered from employers' sites that are read at the source.

## Security

- **Login:** one account per computer, made on the first visit and kept in `user/app/account.json` as the name and an argon2 hash of the password (`auth.ts`). The server checks it, and every API call and document needs the session cookie it sets. A login lasts 30 days unused, kept in `user/app/sessions.json` by a hash of its id, so the server starting again (an update) doesn't ask for it again; logging out ends it, and an account made again starts with none. The name is the same name in any case. What a new account needs is in `ui/account-rules.ts`, which the server holds to and the board that makes the account shows as it is typed:
  - The name is what signing in on Pip's site takes too, so it is the site's rule: up to 40 letters (any language), digits, dots, underscores, dashes and spaces, starting with a letter or a digit. While it is typed, the board asks the server, which asks the site, whether another computer has it already.
  - The password: at least 8 characters, not one of the 10,000 most common passwords, and without the name in it. `public/common-passwords.txt` holds the common ones of 8 characters or more, in lower case, from [SecLists](https://github.com/danielmiessler/SecLists)' `10k-most-common.txt` (MIT License, Copyright (c) 2018 Daniel Miessler). No mix of kinds of characters is asked for: it makes a password harder to remember, not harder to guess.
- The server listens on this computer only. It checks the Host and Origin headers and requires a cookie, so other websites cannot reach it.
- `/open` and `/update` answer only with the server's key (`user/app/token`), which the launcher and a Mac's scripts read from the disk: where to open Pip, and, for an installed Pip updating itself, whether it is busy and that it is about to update (installer/README.md, Updates).
- `policy.ts` decides what a session may do without asking, by the access picked for each conversation in the chat:
  - **Ask me** (the default): reading the project, research, the commands in `.claude/settings.json`, known LaTeX compiles and writing workflow output run on their own, and everything else waits for your approval in the browser.
  - **Automatic in the project**: also every edit inside the project, except the files that change what the agent may do (`.claude/settings`, commands, agents, `web/`, `user/CLAUDE.md`, `user/CV_RULES.md`, the CV checks, any `SKILL.md`). Commands, deletes and anything outside the project still ask.
  - **Full access**: everything runs without asking. Postings are untrusted text, so a pasted posting could then make the agent do anything; the chat warns while it is picked.
  - The deny rules in `.claude/settings.json` hold in every mode, and a daily run always uses Ask me, refusing whatever would ask. Switching a conversation to a more open access lets a request that is already waiting go ahead if the new access allows it.
- There is no ChatGPT key any more: one an earlier version saved in `~/.ai-job-search/ai-keys.json` is deleted, unread, when the server starts. The mail password is kept in that folder, which sessions are denied reading, sealed with DPAPI (`vault.ts`), and the API never returns it.
- Conversations and the access token are stored in `user/app/`, which git ignores.
